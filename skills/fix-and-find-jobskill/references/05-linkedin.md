# Phase 5 — Professional profile alignment

Bring the user's public profile in line with the rewritten CV. Default to
LinkedIn; if their field runs on a different network, work on that one instead
with the same principles.

The point is consistency. A recruiter who reads an application and then looks
the person up must find the same story, told the same way. A profile that
contradicts the CV — different dates, a weaker headline, an achievement that
only appears in one place — costs more than a thin profile does.

## What a profile is not

A profile is not the CV pasted into a web form. Different medium, different
rules:

- It is found through search, so keywords matter more, not less.
- It is read by people who were not looking for it, so the opening must earn
  attention on its own.
- It is public and permanent, so it speaks more broadly than a CV aimed at one
  posting.
- It is first-person. The CV is not.

## Work through it in this order

**1. Headline.** The single highest-value field — it follows the user's name
everywhere on the platform and carries heavy weight in recruiter search. Not
just a job title. Role plus specialty plus the differentiator, loaded with the
terms recruiters actually search in that field. Offer two or three options.

**2. About.** Build it from the phase 2 summary, rewritten in first person and
given room to breathe. Open with a line that earns the click, then the evidence,
then what they are looking for now. Keep the strongest content in the first two
lines — the rest sits behind "see more".

**3. Experience.** Carry over the XYZ bullets, loosened slightly for a more
conversational register. Dates and titles must match the CV exactly. Any
mismatch is a credibility problem, not a formatting one.

**4. Skills.** Front-load the ATS keywords from phase 1 that the platform
supports. Order matters — the top few are what show first and what endorsements
concentrate on.

**5. Everything else the field values.** Certifications and licences, education,
publications, portfolio links, recommendations, languages. Include what that
market screens on.

**6. Settings worth checking.** Whether they are open to work and whether that
is visible to recruiters only or publicly. Custom profile URL. Location and
work arrangement matching what they told you in phase 3. Profile photo and
banner present.

## How to work

Draft everything in chat first and let the user approve it before anything is
written to the live profile. This is their public professional identity — never
edit it silently.

When editing through the browser, go field by field and confirm each change
landed. Profile editors save inconsistently.

Never change their name, never remove existing recommendations, never alter
employment dates to something the user has not confirmed.

## Close the phase

Show what changed and what is left for them to do by hand — a photo, a
recommendation to request, a certification to upload. Then:

> Profile is aligned with the CV.
>
> Want me to check your email for replies and new openings? You can also trigger
> that any time by telling me "check my email".
